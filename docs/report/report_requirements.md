## Our template build (2026-10-20)

- The report is built with `\usepackage[sglblindworkshop,final]{neurips_2026}` and `\workshoptitle{Predictive AI Evaluation Competition (PAIEC)}`: non-anonymous, without line numbers, and with the first-page footer that names the workshop track ("Workshop:" and the workshop title, after the NeurIPS 2026 conference line).
- The style file is not committed. It carries no licence statement, and its header holds the e-mail address of its maintainer. It lives at `tools/report_pdf/neurips_2026.sty`, ignored by git. The build checks its sha256 against the pin in the template row and, if the file is missing, stops with the download URL and the pin.
- The style's font sizes and geometry are used as they are. Under XeLaTeX a Times-like face (TeX Gyre Termes, or the build's current Times-like font) stands in for the style's `ptm`.
- Open point for the author. The NeurIPS instructions reserve `final` for papers accepted to NeurIPS and ask that submissions omit it. Without `final`, `sglblindworkshop` stays non-anonymous, but the style then prints the footer "Submitted to ... Do not distribute.", numbers the lines and hides the acknowledgments. The competition page does not say which form it expects; the organisers can be asked (last paragraph of "What is not stated anywhere I could read").

## What this means for the draft

1. The abstract does not fit the form. At dd3e372 the draft's abstract is 5,236 characters (882 words, Markdown included), and OpenReview takes at most 5,000. The OpenReview abstract needs cutting regardless of what the PDF's abstract says. A NeurIPS-style abstract (about 150 to 250 words) would also match how the E&D criteria read a paper (below). *2026-10-20:* the NeurIPS instructions limit the abstract to one paragraph; report v2's abstract is one paragraph.
2. Format and length are set (read 2026-10-20). The report uses the NeurIPS template, with approximately 4 to 8 pages of main text; references and appendices are excluded and have no page limit. The author's rule (2026-10-20) is at most 8 pages, aiming at 7.5 to 8, checked by `--strict` (`docs/report/style_guide.md`, rule 10). The main text must stand alone: the NeurIPS instructions call the appendix optional reading, so the evidence for every claim the main text makes stays in the main text. Report v2's main text, cut to plan P2's 9,000-word rule (8,994 words at v1.1.0, `docs/release.md` section 10.1), is too long for 8 pages of the template. The author's decisions of 2026-10-20 keep in the main text the hidden-run reading (Table 1 and Figure 2), the submitted predictor and the regime check (Figure 3), with one merged table of regimes and baselines. The pool evaluation becomes one paragraph without a table (Table 5 moves to App C.6), and the gate figure (Figure 1) and the conduct details move to the appendices, with pointers. The report is still graded on criteria "adapted from" the NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations & Datasets guidelines; that track's own rules (a 9-page main text, double-blind review) do not apply. *2026-10-02 reading, kept for the record:* no page limit or template was stated, so format was free and length not policed, and a short main text with long appendices in the NeurIPS style was the safe reading. The draft then ran about 31,700 words in Markdown, and the review's P2.19 trim was the lever. The PDF must be built from the Markdown before submission.
3. Names, not anonymity. The page says so since the reading of 2026-10-20 ("Technical reports do not need to be anonymous."), and the build's `sglblindworkshop` option prints the author block. The title block names the sole author, Nikita L. Polomoshnov, Moscow State University, nikitapol@fbb.msu.ru; every author needs an OpenReview profile before the form can be submitted. Do not anonymise. A double-blind version is neither asked for nor possible on this form. *2026-10-02:* the page was silent, the form not blind in practice, and the title block read `Authors: TODO(team)`.
4. The code link is mandatory for presentation consideration. The title block links the public repository, https://github.com/doctawho42/PAIEC, as the code link; the 2026-10-02 draft had `Code: TODO(link to the public release)` there. The report must link every released item. Training data other than measurement-db: §8.6 of report v1 says none was used; on 2026-10-20 the page asks for "any permitted training data that differ from measurement-db". The row export (data/release_rows, §8.4 of report v1) and the LLM features (data/features/) are derived research data. Say in the report whether and where they are released, since the organisers will "review and rerun the released method".
5. Rerunnability is graded explicitly. §8 of report v1 already holds the rebuild and rerun commands. Keep them in the PDF, with wall times; an appendix is fine, and since 2026-10-20 the page says appendices have no page limit.
6. The disclosures the page asks for are present in §8.6 and §8.2 of report v1: training data (measurement-db only); pretrained models (Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Qwen3-14B-AWQ, research only); the LLM coding assistant (Claude Code); and every formative-feedback use. One conduct point the team should read against the rules: §8.6's inventory scan cloned repositories of the 161-benchmark inventory, which includes test-pool candidates. The rules say participants "must use benchmarks in the public training pool for competition-specific training and curation", and must not "extract or share private test data". The draft discloses the scan in full. Whether to state outright that nothing from it entered training or the submission is for the team to decide. *2026-10-20:* the page no longer has the first of those two sentences. It now permits every data source listed in the benchmark inventory for competition-specific training, development and curation, and forbids sources outside it (row "training data"); the second sentence stands. Report v2 says in App F.8 and App G.6 that the rules restrict competition-specific training and curation to the public pool, which follows the 2026-10-02 wording. Whether to reword those two places is for the author.
7. Give the selected Codabench submission ID in the OpenReview form (read 2026-10-20). The required field `codabench_submission_id` takes digits only, and the ID must correspond to the method the report describes, that is the submission of archive-3, the final submission (`docs/release.md` section 10.2). The page asks for no e-mail. *2026-10-02 reading:* e-mail the organisers with the selected Codabench submission, a step separate from OpenReview with the same deadline. `docs/release.md` section 8 still lists that e-mail among the steps after publishing.
8. Timing. The OpenReview form opened 2026-09-15. Its due date is 2026-10-31 11:59 UTC; the form's expiry is 30 minutes later, but do not plan on that. Codabench's competition phase ends at the same instant. 28 days remain from the date read. *2026-10-20:* the due date, the expiry and the phase end are unchanged, and about 11 days remain.

## What is not stated anywhere I could read

On 2026-10-20 the competition page states the template, the length (approximately 4 to 8 pages of main text, references and appendices without a limit) and that reports need not be anonymous. Still not stated: which of the template's options a report should use (in particular whether `final` is expected, "Our template build" above), whether the NeurIPS paper checklist is expected, required section headings, a camera-ready stage, a poster or slide format, and how the report is weighted against Brier ALC in the summative score. The form still has no separate supplementary upload. *Before 2026-10-20* this list also held the page or word limit, the template, the font and margins, and the anonymity rules.

The organisers can be asked through the contact address on the competition page (Participation, Community & Support), and they offer office hours by appointment.
